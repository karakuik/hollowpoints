Let me be honest upfront: I started aim training because I was getting clipped by people I felt like I should've beaten. Classic ego move. Six months later, here's the reality check.

## What actually improved

Flicking to targets got noticeably cleaner. Not dramatically — I'm not a god now — but the shots I should be hitting, I'm hitting more consistently. Micro-adjustments during tracking also improved. Winning gun fights that previously came down to who had better spray control.

The biggest surprise: I got *calmer*. Something about spending 20 minutes every morning just clicking on dots is meditative in a weird way. I tilt less. I reset faster after dying.

## What didn't transfer

Scenarios. I spent too long in gridshot and Gridshot Precision, which don't map well to how Warzone actually plays. Enemies don't materialize in a fixed range in front of you. They peek from cover, they strafe, they're 150m away and barely visible.

What actually transfers: tracking scenarios, Smoothbot, and — weirdly — the micro-flick scenarios where the target is moving slightly before you click it.

## The regret

I wish I'd spent more time just *playing* in the first month instead of exclusively training. Aim training is supplementary, not a replacement. The game sense, the positioning, reading rotations — that only comes from reps in actual lobbies.

## My current routine

15 minutes before I play. Warm-up playlist in Aimlabs, nothing fancy. Then straight into games.

If you're looking for a starting point: [Gridshot Ultimate](/) for a few minutes, then Smoothbot 180 until it feels easy, then log in. Keep it short and stop obsessing over your score.

The players beating you probably aren't doing aim training. They're just playing more.
